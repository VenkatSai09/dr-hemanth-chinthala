Pancreatitis

Pancreatitis is inflammation of the pancreas, the gland behind the stomach that makes digestive enzymes and insulin. It can be sudden (acute) or long-lasting (chronic).

Acute pancreatitis

It usually causes severe pain in the upper abdomen, often spreading to the back, with vomiting. This needs urgent hospital care.

The most common causes are gallstones and alcohol. Others include very high triglycerides (a blood fat), some medicines and, occasionally, a procedure such as ERCP.

Treatment is with fluids, pain relief and nutrition, and by treating the cause: for example ERCP for a stone blocking the bile duct, and removal of the gallbladder after gallstone pancreatitis. Most people recover within a week. Severe attacks may need intensive care, and fluid collections that form later can sometimes be drained with EUS.

Chronic pancreatitis

Repeated or long-term inflammation slowly damages the pancreas. It can cause:

  • Repeated or constant belly pain.
  • Weight loss and oily, foul-smelling stools, because food is not digested properly.
  • Diabetes.

Causes include alcohol, smoking and genetic factors. A form seen in South India in people who do not drink, called tropical or idiopathic chronic pancreatitis, is also common.

Treatment includes stopping alcohol and smoking, enzyme capsules taken with meals, diabetes care, pain management, and endoscopic treatment (ERCP) for stones or narrowings in the pancreatic duct. Some people need surgery.

Common questions

Is pancreatitis an emergency?

Acute pancreatitis is. Severe upper belly pain with vomiting needs hospital care the same day.

Can I drink alcohol after pancreatitis?

No. Alcohol can trigger new attacks and speed up damage to the pancreas.

Why do I need enzyme capsules?

A damaged pancreas makes too few digestive enzymes. The capsules, taken with meals, help you digest food, gain weight and reduce oily stools.
