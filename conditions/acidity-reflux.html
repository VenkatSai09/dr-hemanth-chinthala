Acidity and acid reflux (GERD)

Acid reflux is when stomach acid flows back up into the food pipe, causing a burning feeling in the chest and a sour taste. Most people have it now and then. When it happens often, it is called gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, or GERD.

Symptoms

  • Heartburn: burning behind the breastbone, often after meals or at night.
  • Food or sour liquid coming back up, and sour belching.
  • Cough, a hoarse voice or a feeling of a lump in the throat.

Chest pain with sweating, breathlessness or pain spreading to the arm or jaw can be a heart problem. Call 108 or go to an emergency department.

Common triggers

A weak valve at the bottom of the food pipe, a hiatus hernia, excess weight, large or late meals, spicy, fried and fatty food, tea, coffee, alcohol, smoking and some medicines, such as painkillers.

When to see a gastroenterologist

  • Symptoms more than twice a week, or not helped by antacids.
  • Difficulty or pain when swallowing.
  • Vomiting blood, or black stools.
  • Weight loss, or new symptoms after the age of 50.
  • Taking acid tablets for months without a review.

Tests

Upper GI endoscopy looks at the food pipe and stomach. A 24-hour pH study measures how much acid comes up, and high-resolution manometry checks how the food pipe moves, for example before surgery or when swallowing is difficult. Dr Chinthala performs these tests.

Treatment

  • Smaller meals, and dinner at least three hours before bed.
  • Raising the head end of the bed.
  • Losing excess weight and stopping smoking.
  • Acid-reducing medicines for a set course, with a plan to step down.
  • Endoscopic or surgical treatment for selected patients.

Common questions

Is it safe to take acid tablets for years?

They are safe for many people, but long-term use should be reviewed. Often the dose can be reduced or stopped once the cause is treated.

Could my acidity be a heart problem?

Sometimes heart pain feels like acidity. Chest pain with sweating, breathlessness or pain in the arm or jaw needs emergency care.

Does spicy food cause ulcers?

No. Most ulcers come from H. pylori infection or painkillers. Spicy food can make reflux symptoms worse, though.
