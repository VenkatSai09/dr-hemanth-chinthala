Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)

EUS combines endoscopy and ultrasound. A small ultrasound probe at the tip of the scope sits right next to the pancreas, bile duct and the wall of the gut, so it gives some of the most detailed pictures available, and it can take samples through a fine needle.

When EUS is used

  • Cysts or lumps in the pancreas found on a CT or MRI scan.
  • Small stones in the bile duct when other scans are unclear.
  • Staging cancers of the food pipe, stomach, pancreas and rectum, to see how deep they go and plan treatment.
  • Lumps under the lining of the gut, which ordinary endoscopy cannot see into.
  • Needle biopsies (EUS-FNA or FNB) to get a diagnosis without surgery.
  • Some treatments, such as draining fluid collections after severe pancreatitis.

How it is done

EUS is done under sedation. The scope is passed through the mouth, or through the back passage to look at the rectum. A scan usually takes 20 to 60 minutes, a little longer if samples are taken. Taking a sample with the needle is not painful.

Before the procedure

  • Nothing to eat for about six hours before.
  • Tell the team about blood thinners and diabetes medicines; some may need to be paused before a biopsy.
  • Bring your earlier scans and reports, and someone to take you home.

Risks

A diagnostic EUS is very safe; a sore throat is the most common after-effect. When a needle sample is taken there is a small risk of bleeding, infection or pancreatitis. Dr Chinthala will discuss this with you.

Common questions

How is EUS different from a CT or MRI scan?

CT and MRI look from outside the body. EUS looks from inside the stomach and duodenum, right next to the pancreas and bile duct, so it can show smaller details and take samples.

Will I be awake?

You are given sedation, so you are comfortable and usually remember little of it.

When will I get biopsy results?

Usually within a few working days. Dr Chinthala will go through them with you and explain the next steps.
