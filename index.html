§ 01 The practice

About the practice

Most digestive problems are solved by listening closely, testing only what is needed, and explaining the result in words that make sense. That is the whole approach — backed by twelve years of training and practice across two of India's most demanding teaching hospitals.

Diagnosis and therapy, in one pair of hands

Dr Chinthala performs both diagnostic and therapeutic endoscopy — from routine gastroscopy and colonoscopy to ERCP for bile-duct stones and EUS for the pancreas. The specialist who sees you in clinic is the one who performs your procedure.

A particular interest in IBD

His research centres on Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis and intestinal tuberculosis. He is trained in intestinal ultrasound — a scan that tracks bowel inflammation without sedation or radiation.

Second opinions, welcome

If you have been told you need a procedure, or a diagnosis doesn't fit how you feel, bring your reports and images. You will leave with a clear view of the options.

— the bile highway

Absorbs water and forms stool. Most bowel cancers begin as small polyps — which can be found and removed during a colonoscopy.

§ 04 In the endoscopy suite

Ten procedures, explained in a sentence each.

  1. 01

    Upper GI endoscopy

    A thin camera examines the food pipe, stomach and first part of the small intestine.

  2. 02

    Colonoscopy

    A look at the full large bowel — to find the cause of symptoms, and to remove polyps before they become a problem.

  3. 03

    Image-enhanced endoscopy

    Special light and magnification that reveal early, subtle changes ordinary white light can miss.

  4. 04

    ERCP

    Treats the bile and pancreatic ducts from the inside — clearing stones and placing stents across blockages, without open surgery.

  5. 05

    Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)

    An ultrasound probe on the tip of the scope gives a close, detailed view of the pancreas and bile ducts.

  6. 06

    GI bleed management

    Finding the source of bleeding and stopping it endoscopically — clips, bands and injection.

  7. 07

    Capsule endoscopy

    A swallowed camera capsule photographs the small intestine, the part a standard scope cannot reach.

  8. 08

    Balloon-assisted enteroscopy

    Reaches deep into the small bowel to take biopsies and treat what the capsule found.

  9. 09

    Intestinal ultrasound

    A non-invasive scan of the bowel wall to monitor Crohn's and colitis — no sedation, no radiation.

  10. 10

    Manometry & 24-hour pH study

    Measures how the food pipe squeezes, and how much acid refluxes over a full day.

§ 08 When to see a gastroenterologist

Symptoms worth a conversation.

  • Heartburn or acidity more than twice a week

    Especially if antacids no longer help, or it wakes you at night.

  • Difficulty or pain when swallowing

    Food that sticks on the way down should always be checked.

  • Bloating, pain or a change in bowel habit

    Lasting more than a few weeks, or not settling with simple changes.

  • Blood in the stool, or black, tar-like stools

    Black stools can mean bleeding higher up in the gut.

    Seek care promptly

  • Vomiting blood or “coffee-ground” material

    This needs same-day medical attention.

    Seek care promptly

  • Yellowing of the eyes or skin

    Jaundice can signal a liver problem or a blocked bile duct.

    Seek care promptly

  • Weight loss or poor appetite without reason

    Unplanned weight loss deserves an explanation.

  • Iron-deficiency anaemia with no clear cause

    The gut is a common, silent source of blood loss.

  • New symptoms after 50, or a family history

    Of bowel or stomach cancer — screening may be advised.

None of these on its own means something serious — but each is a good reason to be examined rather than to wait. Signs marked in orange need prompt attention.

§ 09 Your first visit

What to bring, and what happens next.

  1. i.

    Bring your history

    Previous endoscopy or colonoscopy reports, scans and the CDs that came with them, recent blood tests, and a list of the medicines you take — including supplements.

  2. ii.

    An unhurried consultation

    Your symptoms, history and reports are reviewed together, followed by an examination. You can speak in English, Telugu, Hindi or Tamil.

  3. iii.

    Only the tests that matter

    If a procedure is needed, you will be told why, what it involves and how to prepare — including any fasting or bowel preparation.

  4. iv.

    A plan you can follow

    Treatment, diet and lifestyle advice in writing, with a clear follow-up date — so you always know the next step.

§ 10 Questions

Asked often.

Where and when does Dr Chinthala see patients?

At Medicover Hospital, Myscape Road, beside The District building, Financial District, Nanakramguda, Hyderabad 500032 — Monday to Saturday, 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM.

How do I book an appointment?

Use the request form below, call 040 6833 4455, or walk in to the Medicover Financial District reception. The consultation fee is ₹1,000.

Which conditions does he treat?

Disorders of the food pipe, stomach, intestines, liver, pancreas and bile ducts — including acidity, GI bleeding, hepatitis and cirrhosis, pancreatitis, IBS, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis and intestinal infections such as tuberculosis.

Can I come for a second opinion?

Yes. Bring all previous reports, biopsy results and imaging. Dr Chinthala will review the diagnosis and treatment plan and explain the options available to you.

Does he treat Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis?

Yes — inflammatory bowel disease is a particular focus of his clinical work and research. Treatment is individualised, and progress can be monitored with intestinal ultrasound as well as endoscopy.

How are pancreatic problems diagnosed?

With a combination of blood tests, imaging and, where needed, endoscopic ultrasound (EUS), which gives the closest view of the pancreas without surgery.

Will I get advice on diet and lifestyle?

Yes. Diet, exercise and daily habits matter for most digestive conditions, and you will receive guidance tailored to your diagnosis.
