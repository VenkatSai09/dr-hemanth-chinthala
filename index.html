How he works

Most digestive problems can be solved by listening carefully, testing only what is needed and explaining the result in plain words. Dr Chinthala has worked this way for twelve years, through training at PGIMER and CMC Vellore and now at Medicover.

One doctor from clinic to procedure

He performs diagnostic and therapeutic endoscopy, from gastroscopy and colonoscopy to ERCP for bile duct stones and EUS for the pancreas. The doctor who examines you in clinic is the one who does your procedure.

A research interest in IBD

His research is on Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis and intestinal tuberculosis. He is trained in intestinal ultrasound, a scan that tracks bowel inflammation without sedation or radiation.

Second opinions

If you have been told you need a procedure, or a diagnosis doesn't match how you feel, bring your reports and images. He will go through the options with you.

Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) inflames the lining of the gut, usually in flares that come and go. It is a focus of Dr Chinthala's clinical work and research. Scroll to see what a flare does to the lining, and what healing looks like.

Stage 1

A healthy lining

Smooth, glossy and pink, with fine blood vessels showing through. It absorbs water and nutrients and keeps gut bacteria where they belong.

Stage 2

During a flare

The lining turns red and swollen, the vessels disappear from view and ulcers form. Common signs are diarrhoea, often with blood or mucus, belly pain, an urgent need to go, tiredness and weight loss. Ulcerative colitis affects the colon. Crohn's disease can affect any part of the gut.

Stage 3

Healed, in remission

With the right treatment the ulcers heal and the lining recovers. Doctors call this mucosal healing. It lowers the chance of future flares, hospital stays and surgery, so treatment aims for a healed lining, not only for feeling better.

Procedures he performs

  • Upper GI endoscopy

    A thin camera examines the food pipe, stomach and first part of the small intestine.

  • Colonoscopy

    An examination of the whole large bowel, to find the cause of symptoms and to remove polyps.

  • Image-enhanced endoscopy

    Special light and magnification show early changes that ordinary white light can miss.

  • ERCP

    Treats the bile and pancreatic ducts from inside the gut, clearing stones and placing stents across blockages without open surgery.

  • Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)

    An ultrasound probe on the tip of the scope gives a close view of the pancreas and bile ducts.

  • GI bleed treatment

    Finding the source of bleeding and stopping it with clips, bands or injections.

  • Capsule endoscopy

    You swallow a camera capsule that photographs the small intestine, which a standard scope cannot reach.

  • Balloon-assisted enteroscopy

    Reaches deep into the small bowel to take biopsies and treat what the capsule found.

  • Intestinal ultrasound

    A scan of the bowel wall that monitors Crohn's disease and colitis, with no sedation and no radiation.

  • Manometry and 24-hour pH study

    Measures how the food pipe contracts and how much acid comes back up over a day.

Liver disease, stage by stage

Liver damage usually builds up quietly over years. Scroll to see how a healthy liver changes, and where treatment can still turn it around.

Stage 1

A healthy liver

Smooth and reddish-brown. It filters the blood, stores energy, makes bile and the proteins that help blood to clot.

Stage 2

Fatty liver

Fat builds up inside the liver cells, most often because of alcohol, excess weight or diabetes, and the liver becomes larger and paler. There are often no symptoms. At this stage the damage can usually be reversed by treating the cause.

Stage 3

Cirrhosis

Years of injury from alcohol, hepatitis B or C, or fat replace healthy tissue with scar and nodules. The liver shrinks, hardens and works less well. Treatment aims to stop it getting worse and to manage complications such as bleeding from varices and fluid in the abdomen.

When to see a gastroenterologist

Any one of these is a good reason to get checked. The ones marked "Same day" need prompt attention.

  • Heartburn or acidity more than twice a week

    Especially if antacids no longer help, or it wakes you at night.

  • Difficulty or pain when swallowing

    Food that sticks on the way down should always be checked.

  • Bloating, pain or a change in bowel habit

    If it lasts more than a few weeks, or simple changes do not settle it.

  • Same day

    Blood in the stool, or black, tar-like stools

    Black stools can mean bleeding higher up in the gut.

  • Same day

    Vomiting blood or material like coffee grounds

    This needs medical attention the same day.

  • Same day

    Yellowing of the eyes or skin

    Jaundice can come from a liver problem or a blocked bile duct.

  • Weight loss or poor appetite without a reason

    Unplanned weight loss should be explained.

  • Iron-deficiency anaemia with no clear cause

    Slow bleeding in the gut is a common cause that people do not notice.

  • New symptoms after 50, or a family history of bowel or stomach cancer

    Screening may be advised.

Your first visit

  1. 1

    What to bring

    Earlier endoscopy or colonoscopy reports, scans and their CDs, recent blood tests, and a list of everything you take, including supplements.

  2. 2

    The consultation

    He goes through your symptoms, history and reports with you, then examines you. You can speak in English, Telugu, Hindi or Tamil.

  3. 3

    Tests, if you need them

    If you need a procedure, you will be told why, what it involves and how to prepare, including any fasting or bowel preparation.

  4. 4

    A written plan

    You leave with treatment, diet and lifestyle advice in writing, and a date for your follow-up.

Which conditions does he treat?

Problems of the food pipe, stomach, intestines, liver, pancreas and bile ducts. These include acidity, GI bleeding, hepatitis and cirrhosis, pancreatitis, IBS, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis and intestinal infections such as tuberculosis.

Can I come for a second opinion?

Yes. Bring all earlier reports, biopsy results and scans. Dr Chinthala will review the diagnosis and the treatment plan and explain your options.

Does he treat Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis?

Yes. Inflammatory bowel disease is a focus of his clinical work and research. Treatment is planned for each patient, and progress can be checked with intestinal ultrasound as well as endoscopy.

How are pancreatic problems diagnosed?

With blood tests, imaging and, when needed, endoscopic ultrasound (EUS), which gives the closest view of the pancreas without surgery.

Will I get advice on diet and lifestyle?

Yes. Diet, exercise and daily habits affect most digestive conditions, and you will get advice that fits your diagnosis.
